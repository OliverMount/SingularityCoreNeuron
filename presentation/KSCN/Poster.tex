            \begin{block}{\LARGE Introduction}
            \justifying
        {\large 1. In research on perceptual awareness, theories, such as the global neuronal workspace theory (GWT) and the information integration theory (IIT), have postulated different neural mechanisms and brain regions underlying the temporal dynamics and processing of  stimuli. \\[0.3 cm]
2. However, no studies have yet mechanistically modeled and validated these theoretical frameworks.\\[0.3 cm]
3. To address this gap, we conducted an EEG-based analysis of neural representations related to the orientations of backward-masked Gabor stimuli. Additionally, we developed a large-scale dynamic model to elucidate the underlying mechanisms of stimulus representations across different states of awareness.
               }
            \end{block}

        \justifying
        In visible conditions, robust dynamic neural representations in sensory and central areas suggest that this mechanisms may be sufficient to support initial stages of conscious awareness.


        During invisible conditions, with weak dynamic coding, early stable neural representations of the stimulus emerge from the frontal cortex, implicating a potential role of frontal recurrent neural circuit for conscious awareness.
